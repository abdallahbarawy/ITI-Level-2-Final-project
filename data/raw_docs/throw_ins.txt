THROW-INS

A throw-in is the method of restarting play when the whole of the ball passes over a touchline, either on the ground or in the air. It is awarded to the opponents of the team that last touched the ball, and it is taken from the point where the ball crossed the line. A throw-in is perhaps the most misjudged restart in the game, because its technique is strictly defined by Law 15 and errors are punished immediately by awarding the throw to the other team.

To take a legal throw-in, the player must face the field of play, have part of each foot on the ground either on or behind the touchline, and use both hands to deliver the ball from behind and over the head. The ball is in play as soon as it enters the field of play, and it must be thrown from the exact point where it left the pitch. If the player lifts a foot, steps onto the field before releasing the ball, or delivers it with one hand or below the head, the referee awards a foul throw and the throw-in passes to the opposition. A player taking a throw-in cannot touch the ball again until it has been touched by another player, and a goal cannot be scored directly from a throw-in. If the ball enters the opponent's goal directly from a throw-in, a goal kick is awarded; if it enters the thrower's own goal, a corner kick is awarded.

Opponents must stand at least two metres from the point of the throw-in, and a player who unfairly distracts or impedes the thrower may be cautioned. A goalkeeper cannot handle the ball directly from a team-mate's throw-in, in the same way that they cannot handle a deliberate back-pass.

Tactically, throw-ins are more important than many spectators realise. In the attacking third, a long throw delivered into the penalty area is almost as dangerous as a corner, and specialists such as Rory Delap at Stoke City turned this into a genuine weapon. Quick throw-ins are used to restart play before the opposition can organise their defensive shape, while short throw-ins help teams keep possession and build attacks patiently. Because there is no offside directly from a throw-in, attackers can position themselves freely in advanced areas before the ball is delivered, which makes well-drilled throw-in routines a valuable source of chances.
