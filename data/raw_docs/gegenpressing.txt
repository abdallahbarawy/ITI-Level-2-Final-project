GEGENPRESSING

Gegenpressing is the German term for counter-pressing, the tactic of hunting the ball immediately after losing possession in order to win it back as quickly as possible. It was developed and popularised by coaches such as Ralf Rangnick, Jürgen Klopp and Thomas Tuchel, and it became the defining style of Klopp's Borussia Dortmund and Liverpool sides. Klopp famously described counter-pressing as the best playmaker in the world, because winning the ball back near the opponent's goal creates high-quality chances before the opposition can reorganise defensively.

The central idea is that the moment a team loses the ball is the moment the opponent is most vulnerable. Instead of retreating into a defensive shape, the players nearest the ball swarm it, closing down the ball carrier from several angles and cutting off the nearest passing options. The reaction must be almost instant, typically within five to six seconds, and it must be collective: one player presses the ball while team-mates mark the obvious outlets. If the ball is not recovered quickly, the team then drops into its normal defensive block.

Gegenpressing relies on specific triggers that tell the team when to press. Common triggers include a backward pass by the opponent, a heavy or loose first touch, a pass into a player facing their own goal, or a poor clearance. Because the pressing happens high up the pitch, the defensive line must push up to stay compact, which in turn requires a goalkeeper comfortable sweeping behind the defence. A concept known as rest defence describes how the remaining players position themselves while the team attacks, so that if possession is lost they can immediately press or defend the counter.

The benefits are clear: sustained pressure suffocates opponents, prevents them from playing out from the back, and turns turnovers into shots and goals. The costs are equally real. Gegenpressing is physically exhausting, so it demands exceptional fitness and squad depth for rotation. If the first press is broken, there is often large space behind a high defensive line for fast opponents to exploit. It also requires enormous tactical discipline, since one player pressing alone can be bypassed easily; the system only works when every player acts together.
