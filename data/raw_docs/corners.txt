CORNERS

A corner kick is awarded when the whole of the ball passes over the goal line, on the ground or in the air, having last been touched by a defending player, and a goal is not scored. The kick is taken from the corner arc nearest to where the ball crossed the goal line. The ball must be stationary inside the corner arc, and it is in play as soon as it is kicked and clearly moves; it does not need to leave the arc. Opponents must retreat at least 9.15 metres (ten yards) from the corner arc until the ball is in play. A goal can be scored directly from a corner kick, which is sometimes called an Olympic goal.

The corner taker may not touch the ball a second time until it has been touched by another player. Offside does not apply directly from a corner kick, so attackers can station themselves close to the goalkeeper and defenders without being flagged. However, once the first contact is made, offside applies to the subsequent phase of play, which is why attacking players must be alert during rebounds and second balls.

Corners are a major source of goals, and teams invest heavily in set-piece routines. Delivery is usually described as an inswinger, which curves towards the goal, or an outswinger, which curves away from it. An inswinger is often aimed at the near post or the penalty spot, while an outswinger is frequently targeted at the far post for attackers arriving late. A short corner is a deliberate pass to a nearby team-mate, used to create a better crossing angle or to draw defenders out of the penalty area before delivering the ball. Some teams also play the corner short and then work the ball into the box through a rehearsed passing pattern.

Defensive organisation at corners is equally detailed. Teams choose between zonal marking, where defenders are assigned areas to attack the ball, man-marking, where each defender tracks a specific opponent, or a hybrid of the two. Most sides place one or two players on the posts and leave one or two forwards high up the pitch as a counter-attacking threat. A goalkeeper who is confident claiming crosses can relieve huge pressure, while a poor decision to come for the ball can gift a goal. Because corners combine technique, timing, physicality and rehearsed movement, they reward preparation as much as talent.
